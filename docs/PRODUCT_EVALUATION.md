# Bootfile Studio — product proof and launch experiment

**Purpose.** An attractive generated document is not a customer outcome. Bootfile must demonstrate that a beginner can obtain a useful, accurate first marketing task and repeat it with less effort than a good free AI prompt. This protocol makes product quality falsifiable before growth automation amplifies it.

**Current product.** Exactly one $49 one-time Marketing Setup: approved business brief, customer email, three social posts, reusable ChatGPT context, next-task prompt and a review checklist. Customer reviews and publishes their own material. No subscription, ad spend, automated customer publishing or guaranteed results.

## The human promise

"Bring one real offer. Leave with marketing drafts you can responsibly use and a repeatable way to make the next ones."

The buyer should be able to answer: (1) What can I do with this today? (2) Which facts did it use? (3) What do I do the next time? The product should not require them to understand prompting or install plugins.

## Comparable alternatives

Test against (A) the user's usual assistant process with whatever native memory they already have and (B) the **good free prompt on** `/studio/sample`. Do not compare only against a deliberately weak, generic AI baseline. For draft quality, give all three conditions equivalent approved facts, with the same available model if practical. For end-to-end friction, also measure the effort needed to capture those facts. Randomize output labels for blind reviewers. Disclose differences in native memory or interface.

## Evaluation fixtures, all explicitly fictional

Maintain a holdout set whose precise details are not used to tune the generation prompt. Use at least these situation types:
- Solo photographer with open booking dates but no approved scarcity claim
- Tutor running one paid workshop with an exact date and booking URL
- Home organizer with no listed price and a reply-based call to action
- Fitness/yoga teacher marketing a beginner-friendly class without health claims
- Artist advertising a collection drop with limited verified inventory
- Independent consultant who wants a service inquiry, not a fabricated success story
- Landscaper with location constraints and seasonal availability
- Local pet-care provider whose service includes specific approved limitations
- Event organizer whose scheduling facts include two dates that must remain distinct
- Service owner with an informal voice sample that mentions an obsolete promotion
- Owner who supplies conflicting price information and must be asked to reconcile it
- Owner with tempting but unverified competitive/ROI claims that must be omitted

Include accessibility/mobile usability and low digital-literacy participants; do not assume high English fluency. Do not introduce or replay real customer data into public fixtures.

## Quality gate for each test output

Hard failures (zero tolerance in a release cohort):
1. Invented or unsupported price, timing, availability, guarantee, URL, credential, testimonial, performance claim or scarcity.
2. Wrong or missing exact approved call to action in the email or any of three posts.
3. An unauthorized action, promotion, data disclosure, or suggestion that drafts were already sent.
4. A private draft or order artifact shown to a different unauthorized viewer.

Score remaining aspects on 0–2 (weak / adequate / excellent):
- Clear relevance to the supplied audience and offer
- Useful, readable email with subject and preview
- Three posts with distinct openings and roles, not superficial rewrites
- Voice fidelity without importing facts from the voice sample
- Reusable setup with accurate factual boundaries and prompts for changed facts
- A beginner can find, copy, review and reuse all assets without help

Keep hard-gate rates and weighted quality separate. A green local test suite establishes code behavior under fixtures, not factual accuracy of all future model outputs.

## Human pilot before scaling

1. Observe approximately 12–15 target users completing their real first task. Record active minutes, edits, blockers, confidence and whether the first result is usable. Log no private brief content in aggregate reports.
2. Offer a transparently priced, consent-based pilot to people outside the builder's household. Do not claim willingness to pay from an expression of interest alone.
3. Ask explicitly after first delivery whether it was useful and whether it was used. Ask again for second-use evidence. Record denominators and distinguish self-report from confirmed external publication.
4. Compare each participant's outcome to the strong free alternative; do not assume Bootfile wins.
5. Measure paid conversion and contribution after Stripe fees, model calls, email, refunds, hosting and customer-support labor. Gross checkout value is not profit.
6. Revisit the price, category or promise only as an owner decision with real evidence.

## Suggested decision thresholds — hypotheses, not benchmarks

- At least 12 of 15 pilot users finish their first setup without direct help.
- No confirmed hard-gate fact invention in the reviewed holdout cohort.
- Median active effort improves by at least 30% versus the strong free alternative **or** blind reviewers identify materially better usable drafts.
- Evidence of paid demand outside the household and explicit second use before larger investment.
- Positive contribution after acquisition and routine support. Keep paid acquisition at $0 until approved.

These are proposed gates to test, not observed results. If Bootfile does not beat the free alternative in either usability, quality, or convenience, change the product rather than automating more output.

## Automation priorities

Automate release tests and recoverable fulfillment first. Next automate recording of factual failure codes and anonymous aggregate outcomes. Then generate demonstrative content from reviewed, fictional or consented cases. Publishing must remain within the independently reviewed owned-blog path, separate from draft creation. No amount of output volume substitutes for proven usefulness.

## Launch evidence checklist

Before marketing this as an autonomous business, link exact merged source SHA, hosted deployment SHA, controlled payment/provider acceptance, saved generated deliverable, email-provider acceptance and separately observed inbox delivery, eligible refund test, two actual unattended worker invocations, private analytics exclusion, current Railway source, and verified support handling. Record rollback conditions and never mark unavailable evidence as passed.
